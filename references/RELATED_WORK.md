# Related work and attribution

This project studies the long-time particle-count Fano factor of a noninteracting serial chain with a filled source, an empty drain, and flat endpoint couplings. The resource is the number of single-particle orbitals. The [model and conventions](../science/model.md) distinguish that resource from the dimension of the fermionic Fock space.

The transport identities, the classical least-squares filter, polynomial derivative inequalities, passive synthesis, and logarithmic reflection sum rules are established ingredients. The results developed here are the universal serial-chain bound and matching attainable order

```math
F\ge\frac{1}{18N^2+2},
\qquad
\inf_{N\text{-site serial chains}}F=\Theta(N^{-2}),
```

together with the contact-rate constraint

```math
J\,\frac{\log(1/F)}{1-F}
\le\min(\Gamma_L,\Gamma_R)
```

and an explicit family attaining the required logarithmic growth of contact rate relative to current in order. The comparisons below identify where each inherited ingredient enters and what the cited results establish. Bibliographic records are collected in [references.bib](references.bib).

## Scattering theory and counting statistics

The relation between coherent transmission and current belongs to the Landauer framework: R. Landauer, *Spatial Variation of Currents and Fields Due to Localized Scatterers in Metallic Conduction*, IBM Journal of Research and Development **1**, 223–231 (1957), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1147/rd.13.0223). The connection between transmission amplitudes and retarded Green functions is developed by D. S. Fisher and P. A. Lee, *Relation between conductivity and transmission matrix*, Physical Review B **23**, 6851–6854 (1981), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.23.6851).

L. S. Levitov, H. Lee, and G. B. Lesovik, *Electron counting statistics and coherent states of electric current*, Journal of Mathematical Physics **37**, 4845–4866 (1996), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.531672), [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9607137v1), derive the long-time counting formula and its binomial limit. Their Eq. (37) gives the energy integral, with energy-independent scattering amplitudes in that presentation. For the energy-dependent transmission used here, Ehrlich and Schaller's [Eq. (1)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.04322v2) states the required form directly. Setting the reservoir occupations to one and zero, and using a real counting field, gives

```math
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac1t\log\mathbb E e^{sK_t}
=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
\log\!\left[1+T(E)(e^s-1)\right]dE.
```

Consequently, with $`\hbar=1`$,

```math
J=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int T(E)\,dE,
\qquad
D=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int T(E)[1-T(E)]\,dE,
\qquad
F=D/J.
```

Here $`D=\lim_{t\to\infty}\operatorname{Var}(K_t)/t`$ is a count-variance rate. A convention for symmetrized electrical noise can instead give $`S(0)=2e^2D`$, with electrical current $`I=eJ`$. In that convention the same dimensionless Fano factor is $`S(0)/(2eI)`$. The factor of two is a convention for the noise spectral density, not an additional fluctuation contribution.

Ya. M. Blanter and M. Büttiker, *Shot noise in mesoscopic conductors*, Physics Reports **336**, 1–166 (2000), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0370-1573(99)00123-4), [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9910158v2), review the scattering expressions and counting-statistics connection. None of these identities is claimed as a new transport formula.

## Meier et al.: correlated ticks and numerical size scaling

F. Meier, Y. Minoguchi, G. Blasi, G. Haack, and M. Huber, *Exponential gain in clock precision using quantum correlated ticks*, [arXiv:2601.10785v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10785v1) (2026), study fermionic clock ticks generated by a coherent chain. Section III and Eq. (6) report an optimized numerical scaling approximately $`F\sim N^{-1.86}`$. The work also develops the role of correlations between ticks, including a logarithmic regime of count variance and its crossover to long-time behavior.

This is the closest physical precedent for the size question. It establishes that optimized coherent chains can strongly outperform the Butterworth example and already suggests an exponent close to two. The [size theorem](../science/size_bound.md) supplies a converse for every admissible serial chain, and the [constructed family](../science/legendre_family.md) supplies an analytic matching order. The present claim concerns the long-time variance rate after taking the long-time limit at each finite size. It does not replace the finite-time correlated-tick analysis.

## Brandner and Saito: coherent uncertainty relations and Butterworth chains

K. Brandner and K. Saito, *Thermodynamic Uncertainty Relations for Coherent Transport*, Physical Review Letters **135**, 046302 (2025), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/6nww-8wcp), [arXiv:2502.07917v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07917v2), derive uncertainty relations for coherent transport. Their Eqs. (24)–(25) give a serial-chain construction with Butterworth transmission. After an irrelevant energy rescaling, it is

```math
T_N(x)=\frac{1}{1+x^{2N}}.
```

For full source and empty drain, elementary integration of this transmission gives

```math
\frac{\int_{\mathbb R}T_N(1-T_N)\,dx}
{\int_{\mathbb R}T_N\,dx}
=\frac{1}{2N}.
```

Thus an explicit finite-chain route to nearly rectangular transmission, and its $`N^{-1}`$ Fano-factor scaling, are established precedents. The present result identifies the optimal exponent over the entire serial-chain class and evaluates a different inherited filter family. It does not introduce the use of classical filter profiles in quantum transport. The entropy-production constraints in the cited work and the contact-rate constraint here charge different physical resources.

## Ehrlich and Schaller: optimized quantum-dot filters

T. Ehrlich and G. Schaller, *Broadband frequency filters with quantum dot chains*, Physical Review B **104**, 045424 (2021), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.104.045424), [arXiv:2103.04322v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.04322v2), construct low-noise energy filters from chains attached to wide-band reservoirs. Their reverse reaction-coordinate mapping also makes the constant-numerator, all-pole transmission structure explicit in Sec. III B.

Section III C discusses several filter objectives. The implemented iterative optimization minimizes $`\beta V S_M/I_M`$ over a symmetric hopping and the voltage at each stage. Table I provides the resulting parameters for 13 dots; Sec. IV studies current fluctuations and thermodynamic performance. The authors explicitly leave faster convergence through improved optimization open.

This work establishes the physical filtering strategy and finite-chain low-noise examples. It does not give a universal asymptotic lower bound on full-bias partition noise or a matching $`N^{-2}`$ theorem. Its Eqs. (1)–(2) are also a direct source for the counting convention used [above](#counting-statistics).

## Malvar and Calôba: the inherited least-squares filter

H. S. Malvar and L. P. Calôba, *Least-squares low-pass filters with nonmonotonic response*, IEEE Transactions on Circuits and Systems **27**, 1270–1272 (1980), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TCS.1980.1084755), [author-provided original](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/Malvar_Caloba.pdf), introduce the relevant least-squares family. Their $`Q_N^a`$ minimizes $`\int_0^1Q(x)^2\,dx`$ among degree-at-most-$`N`$ polynomials of parity $`N`$ with $`Q(1)=1`$. Their transfer magnitude has the form $`1/[1+\varepsilon^2Q(x)^2]`$.

The polynomial used here is exactly that $`Q_N^a`$ family, at $`\varepsilon=1`$, written in Legendre form:

```math
p_N(x)=\frac{2P'_{N+1}(x)}{(N+1)(N+2)}.
```

The identity follows from the unique constrained least-squares problem and the Legendre reproducing kernel; the [filter analysis](../science/legendre_family.md) gives the calculation. The filter and its passband least-squares criterion are inherited. The present analysis controls the distinct full-line ratio $`\int T(1-T)/\int T`$, including both tails, and establishes its optimal order among physical serial chains. The least-squares criterion alone is not an assertion of finite-$`N`$ Fano-factor optimality.

See also B. Rakovich's subsequent [comment](https://doi.org/10.1109/TCS.1982.1085149), IEEE Transactions on Circuits and Systems **29**, 340–342 (1982).

## Blasi et al.: kinetic uncertainty at strong coupling

G. Blasi, R. Ravell Rodríguez, M. Moskalets, R. López, and G. Haack, *Quantum Kinetic Uncertainty Relations in Mesoscopic Conductors at Strong Coupling*, Physical Review Letters **137**, 056302 (2026), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/9xcz-nlqk), [arXiv:2505.13200v5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13200v5), introduce a generalized activity and a quantum kinetic uncertainty relation valid beyond weak coupling. Their Eq. (14) reads

```math
\frac{I_\alpha^2}{S_{\alpha\alpha}}
\le
\frac{(\mathcal A_\alpha^{\rm cross})^2}
{\mathcal A_\alpha^{\rm cross}-\mathcal A_\alpha^{\rm sh}}.
```

In the exactly full/empty two-terminal model here, substitution into their Eqs. (11) and (13) gives $`\mathcal A_\alpha^{\rm cross}=\mathcal A_\alpha^{\rm sh}=J`$. Thus the denominator vanishes and this inequality supplies no positive lower bound on $`F`$ in that ideal limit. This is a specialization of their expressions, not a failure of their bound. The present serial-site and contact-rate statements remain nontrivial under these occupations and address resources not counted by that activity inequality.

## Markov's polynomial derivative inequality

A. A. Markov, *Ob odnom voprose D. I. Mendeleeva* [*On a question by D. I. Mendeleev*], Zapiski Imperatorskoi Akademii Nauk **62**, 1–24 (1890), proves the classical first-derivative inequality. The [historical archive](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/hat/people/markov.html) provides the original and an [English translation by Carl de Boor and Olga Holtz](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/hat/fpapers/markov4.pdf). The work was read in 1889; the original publication record gives 1890.

For a polynomial $`u`$ of degree at most $`m`$,

```math
\|u'\|_{[-1,1]}\le m^2\|u\|_{[-1,1]}.
```

The [lower-bound proof](../science/size_bound.md) applies this established inequality separately to every component of a sublevel set of the nonnegative degree-$`2N`$ polynomial $`q=1/T-1`$. It converts a derivative bound into a lower bound on integrated partition noise. The classical inequality itself is not new, and its $`m^2`$ endpoint scale explains the attainable quadratic size exponent.

## Passive synthesis, Herglotz functions, and Jacobi matrices

Prescribing an insertion-loss profile and realizing it by a passive lossless network is a classical synthesis problem. An original source is S. Darlington, *Synthesis of Reactance 4-Poles Which Produce Prescribed Insertion Loss Characteristics: Including Special Applications to Filter Design*, Journal of Mathematics and Physics **18**, 257–353 (1939), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/sapm1939181257).

The positive-measure representation of Herglotz functions and the relation between orthogonal polynomials, spectral measures, and Jacobi operators are standard. G. Teschl, *Jacobi Operators and Completely Integrable Nonlinear Lattices*, Mathematical Surveys and Monographs **72**, American Mathematical Society (2000), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/surv/072), provides the relevant material in Secs. 2.1 and 2.5 and Appendix B; an [authorized full text](https://www.mat.univie.ac.at/~gerald/ftp/book-jac/jacop.pdf) is available from the author.

The [realization proof](../science/realization.md) specializes these ideas to the required quantum geometry. It verifies an $`N`$-site real nearest-neighbor Hamiltonian with positive hoppings, zero onsite energies, mirror symmetry, and two equal local endpoint couplings, reproducing the specified scalar transmission without additional orbitals. A generic passive-network realization alone would not certify all of those physical restrictions. The project supplies their verification for this construction and does not claim a new general theory of lossless synthesis.

## Bode–Fano reflection budgets and the contact-rate consequence

R. M. Fano, *Theoretical limitations on the broadband matching of arbitrary impedances*, Journal of the Franklin Institute **249**, 57–83 and 139–154 (1950), [Part I DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0016-0032(50)90006-8), [Part II DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0016-0032(50)91101-X), develops logarithmic reflection constraints for passive matching. The original [MIT Research Laboratory of Electronics Technical Report 41](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/5007), dated 2 January 1948, is openly available.

The [contact-rate proof](../science/contact_rate.md) uses this inherited pole/zero logarithmic method. It derives the exact determinant normalization for the endpoint-coupled conductor and obtains

```math
\int_{\mathbb R}-\log[1-T(E)]\,dE
\le2\pi\min(\Gamma_L,\Gamma_R).
```

The transport consequence comes from weighting energy by $`T(E)/\int T`$ and applying convexity of $`g(r)=-\log r/(1-r)`$ to $`r=1-T(E)`$. Its weighted average is $`F`$, yielding the stated inequality between $`J`$, $`F`$, and the contact rates. Both the reflection-budget lineage and the convexity step are explicit; the inequality is not presented as a new broadband matching principle.

At fixed endpoint rate, the inequality requires at least logarithmic current suppression when $`F\to0`$. For the least-squares family, $`F=\Theta(N^{-2})`$ and $`\Gamma/J\sim3\log N`$. This matches the necessary order of the current cost. It does not establish the optimal leading constant or a complete joint size-current-noise frontier.

### Related quantum-interface matching result

M. V. Churilov, *Sharp Passive Broadband Limits for Multiresonator Quantum Memories*, [arXiv:2607.10704v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10704v2) (2026), applies passive admittance methods to quantum-memory interfaces. Equation (17) explicitly gives the logarithmic reflection identity, including the correction from right-half-plane zeros; its fixed-band consequence bounds the maximum reflection amplitude. The work establishes the corresponding infinite-order minimax infimum and finite-order nonattainment for its passive-atom class. Theorem 3 and the discussion leave the optimal finite-order convergence law open. The final dual-measure section proposes a lower-certificate objective with an externally chosen band measure; it does not establish a matching finite-order lower bound.

This is a close quantum-physics use of the same classical reflection budget. Its observable is worst-case reflection on a prescribed band, whereas the present observable is the current-weighted, full-line fermionic partition-noise ratio. The contact-rate claim here therefore rests on that specific observable conversion, not on moving Bode–Fano reasoning into a quantum setting.

## Scope of the contribution

The scientific distinction is the combination of a universal serial-orbital converse, an exactly realizable matching family, and explicit accounting of the contact/current cost. The filter design, Landauer and counting-statistics framework, Markov inequality, and passive matching mathematics remain credited to their established sources.

The bounds do not establish an exponent for arbitrary coherent networks, whose transmission numerators can depend on energy. They also do not establish the optimum at each finite size, a fixed-current advantage with bounded contact rates, or an analogous law for interacting conductors, structured reservoirs, or finite bias windows. These restrictions are part of the theorem statements, rather than consequences of the cited general uncertainty relations.
